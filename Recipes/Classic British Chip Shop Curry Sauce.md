Classic British Chip Shop Curry Sauce should be thick enough to cling to hot chips, not so thick that it sits like paste. This version has the familiar warm curry powder flavour, a small hit of vinegar, and the gentle sweetness you get from apple and onion.

It also makes a quick tray of fries feel like something from a proper chippy, especially when the sauce is hot and glossy.

## Ingredients

- [ ] 2 tablespoons neutral oil
- [ ] 1 medium yellow onion, finely chopped
- [ ] 1 small Granny Smith apple, peeled and grated
- [ ] 2 garlic cloves, minced
- [ ] 1 teaspoon grated fresh ginger
- [ ] 2 tablespoons mild curry powder
- [ ] 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
- [ ] 2 tablespoons plain all-purpose flour
- [ ] 2 1/2 cups (600 ml) vegetable stock, warm
- [ ] 1 tablespoon malt vinegar
- [ ] 2 teaspoons brown sugar
- [ ] 1/2 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste
- [ ] 1/4 teaspoon ground white pepper
- [ ] Pinch of cayenne pepper, optional

## Instructions

1. **Cook the onion and apple:** Heat the oil in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and grated apple, then cook for 6-8 minutes until soft and lightly golden at the edges.
2. **Bloom the spices:** Stir in the garlic, ginger, curry powder, turmeric, and cayenne if using. Cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
3. **Add the flour:** Sprinkle in the flour and stir for 1 minute. The mixture should look thick and pasty, with no dry flour left in the pan.
4. **Whisk in the stock:** Slowly whisk in the warm vegetable stock, adding a little at a time at first to prevent lumps. Keep whisking until the sauce is smooth.
5. **Simmer the sauce:** Add the malt vinegar, brown sugar, salt, and white pepper. Simmer uncovered for 10-12 minutes, stirring often, until thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
6. **Blend and adjust:** Blend with an immersion blender until smooth. Taste and adjust with more salt, vinegar, sugar, or water as needed, then serve hot.

## Notes

- Makes about 3 cups of sauce.
- Best served hot over chips or battered fish.
- For a gluten-free version, replace the flour with a cornstarch slurry.

### Why Apple Belongs Here

Apple may sound odd until you taste the sauce. It melts into the onion and gives the same mellow sweetness you expect from a chip shop tub. It should not taste like applesauce. Peel it and grate it so it cooks down fast.

If you skip the apple, add another teaspoon of brown sugar and a small splash of vinegar at the end. The sauce will still work, but it will taste flatter.

### Getting the Texture Right

The sauce should pour in a thick ribbon and leave a light coating on a spoon. If it looks grainy, it probably needs a few more minutes of simmering so the flour can fully hydrate. If it looks gluey, whisk in hot water 1 tablespoon at a time.

For the smoothest finish, use an immersion blender directly in the pan. A standard blender works too, but let the sauce cool for a few minutes first and vent the lid.

### FAQs

> [!qna] What makes chip shop curry sauce different from Indian curry?
> Chip shop curry sauce is more like a seasoned gravy than a full curry. It usually has curry powder, stock, a little sweetness, and vinegar, so it tastes mild, tangy, and thick enough for chips.

> [!qna] Can I use chicken stock instead of vegetable stock?
> Yes, chicken stock gives a slightly richer flavour. Use vegetable stock if you want the sauce to stay vegan, and choose a good low-salt stock so the seasoning stays under control.

> [!qna] How do I make the sauce smoother?
> Blend it with an immersion blender after simmering. For a very smooth chip shop texture, pass it through a fine sieve and rewarm it gently before serving.

> [!qna] How long does chip shop curry sauce keep?
> Keep it in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. Reheat it in a small pan with a splash of water or stock because it thickens as it cools.
