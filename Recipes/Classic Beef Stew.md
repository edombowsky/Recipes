This classic beef stew recipe combines tender chunks of beef, carrots, and potatoes in a rich, deeply flavorful sauce. It’s the ultimate cold-weather comfort food.

## Ingredients

- [ ] 3 pounds boneless beef chuck, well-marbled, cut into 1½-inch pieces
- [ ] 2 teaspoons salt
- [ ] 1 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- [ ] 3 tablespoons olive oil
- [ ] 2 medium yellow onions, cut into 1-inch chunks
- [ ] 7 cloves garlic, peeled and smashed
- [ ] 2 tablespoons balsamic vinegar
- [ ] 1½ tablespoons tomato paste
- [ ] ¼ cup all-purpose flour
- [ ] 2 cups dry red wine
- [ ] 2 cups beef broth
- [ ] 2 cups water
- [ ] 1 bay leaf
- [ ] ½ teaspoon dried thyme
- [ ] 1½ teaspoons sugar
- [ ] 4 large carrots, peeled and cut into 1-inch chunks on a diagonal
- [ ] 1 pound small white boiling potatoes (baby yukons), cut in half
- [ ] Fresh chopped parsley, for serving (optional)

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 325°F (165°C) and set a rack in the lower middle position.
2. Pat the beef dry and season with the salt and pepper. In a large Dutch oven or other heavy, oven-safe soup pot (see note), heat 1 tablespoon of the olive oil over medium-high heat until hot and shimmering.
3. Brown the meat in 3 batches, about 5 minutes per batch, adding 1 tablespoon of oil for each new batch. To ensure a good sear, don't crowd the pan, and let the meat develop a nice brown crust before turning it with tongs. Transfer the browned meat to a large plate and set aside.
4. Add the onions, garlic, and balsamic vinegar; cook, stirring with a wooden spoon and scraping the brown bits from bottom of the pan, for about 5 minutes. Add the tomato paste and cook for a minute more.
5. Add the beef with its juices back to the pan and sprinkle with the flour. Stir with wooden spoon until the flour is dissolved, 1 to 2 minutes.
6. Add the wine, beef broth, water, bay leaf, thyme, and sugar. Stir with a wooden spoon to loosen any brown bits from the bottom of the pan and bring to a boil. Cover the pot with a lid, transfer to the preheated oven, and braise for 2 hours.
7. Remove the pot from the oven and add the carrots and potatoes. Cover and place back in oven for about an hour more, or until the vegetables are cooked, the broth is thickened, and the meat is tender.
8. Remove and discard the bay leaf, then taste and adjust the seasoning, if necessary. Serve the stew warm, or let it cool and refrigerate until ready to serve. The flavour improves if made at least a day ahead. Reheat, covered, over medium heat. Garnish with fresh parsley, if desired.

## Notes

- If you don’t have a Dutch oven or other oven-safe pot with a lid, you can cook the stew on the stovetop instead. Keep it covered over very low heat, maintaining a gentle simmer. The cooking time will be about the same.
- **Make-Ahead, Storage & Freezing** This beef stew is ideal for making ahead, as the flavours improve after a day in the refrigerator. Prepare the stew as directed, let it cool, then cover and refrigerate for up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop over medium-low heat until hot. To freeze, let the stew cool completely, then transfer it to an airtight, freezer-safe container and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.
- **Can I Make Beef Stew in a Slow Cooker?** Yes, but for best results, begin on the stovetop as directed. Brown the beef, then cook the onions, garlic, and balsamic vinegar. Return the beef to the pot, sprinkle with the flour, and stir until incorporated. Transfer everything to the slow cooker, including the carrots and potatoes, and add the remaining ingredients. Cook on high for 4 to 5 hours, until the beef is tender and the vegetables are cooked through.
- **How to Thicken Beef Stew** Don’t worry if the stew seems thin during cooking; it won’t fully thicken until the very end. If you’d still prefer a thicker sauce once it’s done, mash 2 tablespoons each of softened butter and flour into a smooth paste (this is called a _beurre manié_). Bring the stew to a simmer on the stovetop, then whisk in the paste a little at a time until the sauce reaches your desired consistency.
