Fasolakia is a Greek side of tender green beans slowly braised in olive oil, onion, and tomato. It’s simple and comforting, with a garlic finish and an optional crumble of feta, a lovely change on your Thanksgiving table.

## What Is Fasolakia?

Fasolakia (also called fasolakia lathera) is a classic Greek dish of green beans slowly braised in olive oil, onion, and tomato. It belongs to the lathera family, which means “cooked in oil”: simple vegetable dishes where good olive oil is the star. The beans cook until soft and silky, soaking up a rich tomato sauce. It’s usually served as a side, but with feta and crusty bread it easily becomes a light main.

## With or Without Potatoes?

Many Greek families add potatoes to make fasolakia a heartier vegetarian main. It’s traditional, but leaving them out is just as common. We skip them so the green beans really shine and the dish stays a lighter side.

To add them, cut 2 medium potatoes into chunks and add them with the beans. They cook in the same time but soak up some of the sauce, so add a little more water, about 60 to 120 ml (¼ to ½ cup).

## Ingredients

- [ ] 700 g green beans fresh or frozen, trimmed
- [ ] 400 g chopped tomatoes 1 can
- [ ] 1 large onion finely chopped
- [ ] 3 garlic cloves 2 chopped, 1 finely minced for the end
- [ ] 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- [ ] 80 ml extra virgin olive oil plus a drizzle to finish
- [ ] 120 ml water plus more if needed
- [ ] 1 teaspoon sugar optional, skip with Mutti tomatoes
- [ ] ½ teaspoon dried oregano
- [ ] ½ bunch fresh parsley chopped
- [ ] 1 teaspoon salt
- [ ] black pepper to taste

### Toppings

- [ ] feta crumbled, optional
- [ ] crusty bread to serve

## Instructions

1. Warm the olive oil in a wide, heavy pot or skillet over medium-low heat. Cook the onion gently for about 8 minutes until soft and sweet, not browned. Add the 2 chopped garlic cloves for the last minute.
2. Add the green beans and stir for 2 to 3 minutes until glossy with oil.
3. Stir in the canned tomatoes, tomato paste, sugar (if using), oregano, salt, pepper, and half the parsley.
4. Pour in hot water until it comes about a third of the way up the beans.
5. Cover and cook on low for about 30 minutes at a gentle simmer, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water only if the pot looks dry.
6. Turn off the heat. Stir in the minced garlic, the remaining parsley, and a drizzle of olive oil. Cover and rest for 10 minutes.
7. Serve warm or at room temperature, with feta and crusty bread.

## Notes

- **Don’t skimp on the olive oil.** It’s what makes this a *lathera* dish (“cooked in oil”) and keeps the beans silky.
- **Soft is the goal.** The beans should be tender and olive green, not crisp and bright.
- **Less water is more.** The sauce should cling, not pool.
- **Gentler garlic?** Add the last clove 2 minutes before the end of cooking instead of off the heat.
- **Even better tomorrow.** It keeps 3 to 4 days in the fridge.
- **Variations.** Add 2 cubed potatoes for a heartier main, or swap some parsley for dill or mint.
- **More Variations** Swap some of the parsley for fresh dill or mint, both popular in Greece. A pinch of dried oregano or a bay leaf in the sauce adds a warm, herby note. For a richer finish, crumble feta on top just before serving.
- **Making Ahead and Storing Leftovers** Fasolakia is even better the next day, once the flavours have had time to settle, which makes it perfect to prepare ahead for a holiday meal. Keep leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for 3 to 4 days. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water, or do as the Greeks do and enjoy it at room temperature.
